## 🧭 Coaching Philosophy

### Core Beliefs
- **Every player must learn how to block and tackle.**  
  Fundamentals come first, regardless of position.

- **Offense > Defense (time allocation).**  
  Offense takes more reps and detail to install correctly.

- **No “daddy ball.”**  
  Players earn reps through effort, not last names.

For the stick concept, the "stick" receiver & the QB are reacting to the OLB.

#### Indi Period Structure

##### Overview
The Individual (Indi) period is where players develop position-specific skills that tie directly into that day’s install. Each group should focus on fundamentals that support the offensive concept being installed (e.g., **Power**, **Flood**, **Mesh**, etc.). Goal: every player understands their role within the play and refines the technique that makes it work.

---

##### Quarterbacks (QB)
> Note: QBs open up to play side.  Example: if play is PWR RT, QB under center should be opening their hips to the play side (clock wise)
**Focus:** footwork, timing, reads, and ball placement tied to the day’s install.

**Examples**
- **Power (run) install**
  - Handoff mechanics and footwork (step depth, alignment with RB).
  - Mesh-point timing and play-action footwork.
  - Snap + center exchange under pressure.
- **Flood (pass) install**
  - Drop progressions (3-step, 5-step, rollout mechanics).
  - Read progression: flat → intermediate → deep.
  - Ball placement on the move; rollout accuracy.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Snap and exchange (under center / shotgun)
- Pocket movement / reset
- Quick-release accuracy drills
- Ball security and fake mechanics

---

##### Offensive Line (OL)
**Focus:** stance, first-step, communication, and technique for the blocking scheme being installed.

**Examples**
- **Power install**
  - Down blocks, combo teams, backside pull mechanics.
  - Step progression (gap step → drive step).
  - Timing with pulling guard / lead block.
- **Flood / pass install**
  - Slide/half-slide footwork, protection landmarks for rollouts.
  - Blitz recognition and pickup calls.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Stance & explosive first step
- Hand placement and strike timing
- Finish and drive mechanics
- Zone vs gap rule recognition (covered/uncovered)

---

##### Running Backs (RB)
**Focus:** alignment, mesh, ball security, reads, and pass protection for install plays.

**Examples**
- **Power install**
  - Footwork (open step, crossover, downhill path).
  - Read pulling guard and hit the designated gap.
  - Finish runs with low pad level.
- **Flood install**
  - Pass protection ID and punch/anchor technique.
  - Swing/tunnel route timing with QB rollout.
  - Checkdown timing and spacing.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Ball-security circuits
- Blitz pickup and recognition
- Vision / cutback drills
- Route fundamentals and catch-on-the-move

---

##### Wide Receivers (WR)
**Focus:** releases, route precision, leverage, and blocking technique.

**Examples**
- **Power install**
  - Blocking fundamentals (engage, hand placement, drive feet).
  - Crack/blocking concepts vs safety fits.
- **Flood install**
  - Layered route timing (flat → out → corner).
  - Must Outside Release (MOR) and leverage reads vs DB.
  - Stem adjustments vs press.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Stance and initial burst / release
- Route-tree reps and timing dots
- Catching under pressure and tracking
- Blocking technique and effort-led plays

---

#### Combined Indies
After position-specific work, combine groups to begin integrating the playbook in small units.

- **OL + RBs (Run period)**
  - Execute run blocking and backfield mesh on Power, Counter, Zone.
  - Emphasize timing between pullers and ball-carrier lanes.
  - Coach focus: pad level, aiming points, and run-fit integrity.

- **QBs + WRs (Pass period)**
  - Run route concepts installed that day (e.g., Flood, Mesh, Stick).
  - Focus on timing, spacing, and ball placement.
  - Adjust routes based on coverage shell and QB reads.
  - Work rollout timing and play-action sync when applicable.

- **Situational pairings**
  - OL + QB for protection install (center exchanges, slide calls).
  - RB + WR for chip/pickup and screen timing.

---

#### Team Offense Period
All position groups come together to run the full install at increasing tempo.

**Goals**
- Execute the full concept as a single unit.
- Reinforce communication (line calls, motion adjustments, QB cadence).
- Prioritize quality reps over high quantity.
- Add situational context (down & distance, hash, personnel group).

**Suggested Structure**
1. **Walk-through (10–15 min)**  
   - Alignments, motions, assignments called slowly; coaches correct positioning.
2. **Half-speed reps (8–12 min)**  
   - Work timing and clean execution; stop to correct technique.
3. **Full-speed reps (12–20 min)**  
   - Game-tempo reps with accountability and limited coaching breaks.
4. **Coach review (5–10 min)**  
   - Quick notes, corrections, and specific homework for next Indi period.

## Defensive Period Structure

### Heavy Tackle Session

Before breaking into defensive individual periods, this is a great time to run a **Heavy Tackle Session**.  
This session focuses on tackling fundamentals in a controlled, high-rep environment. It allows players to build confidence and technique before applying it in live or team settings.

#### Goals
- Reinforce **safe and consistent tackling technique**
- Improve **tracking angles** and **contact confidence**
- Teach players to **trust their leverage and pursuit**
- Build team-wide **physicality and effort**

#### Structure
Each station should emphasize **form, control, and repetition**. Rotate through stations every 4–6 minutes depending on team size.

---

### Defensive Line (DL)
**Focus:** stance, get-off, block destruction, pursuit, and gap integrity.

**Examples**
- **Run-fit install (Power/Counter looks)**
  - Key read: near hip of the offensive lineman.
  - Strike and shed: inside hand to sternum, outside arm free.
  - Maintain gap responsibility — don’t chase.
  - Work “down block recognition” and squeeze technique.
- **Pass-rush install**
  - Stance and get-off on cadence.
  - Pass-rush moves: rip, swim, bull, club-rip.
  - Lane integrity — rush with a plan, not chaos.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Get-off drill (ball key reaction)
- Strike and shed with hands
- Gap fit drill (1-on-1 or 2-on-2 fit recognition)
- Pursuit angles and backside containment

---

### Linebackers (LB)
**Focus:** reads, key recognition, footwork, block destruction, and pursuit angles.

**Examples**
- **Run-fit install**
  - Read guards: pull = flow; base = fill.
  - Fit drills (A/B/C gap fits with DL).
  - Shock-and-shed drills vs lead blockers.
  - Leverage pursuit to the ball (inside-out angle).
- **Pass coverage install (Flood, Mesh, etc.)**
  - Drop landmarks (hook-curl, curl-flat, middle read).
  - Open hips and drive downhill on QB eyes.
  - Match routes within zone drops.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Stance, shuffle, read step
- Key read drill (guard read, RB flow)
- Open-field tackle form and tracking
- Zone drop and reaction (coach QB eyes)
- Blitz path recognition / timing

---

### Defensive Backs (DB)
**Focus:** alignment, leverage, footwork, and ball skills in both man and zone coverages.

**Examples**
- **Man coverage install**
  - Press mirror drills (no hands, then with hands).
  - “Eyes, hips, hands” progression — mirror WR release.
  - Trail technique and recovery.
- **Zone install**
  - Landmark drops (flat, curl, deep thirds/halves).
  - Route recognition — understand #1/#2 receiver keys.
  - Break-and-drive drills based on QB eyes.

**Everyday Indi Work**
- Backpedal → break 45° and 90° transitions
- Open hips, T-step, and plant mechanics
- Ball tracking and high-point technique
- Communication and checks (MOFO/MOFC recognition)
- Angle tackle circuit

---

### Combined Indies

After each group masters their individual techniques, pair position groups to connect responsibilities and communication.

- **DL + LB (Run fits)**
  - 2-gap vs 1-gap fits.
  - Gap exchange drills (slant + scrape).
  - LB flow and fit off DL movements.
  - Emphasize “spill vs squeeze” technique.

- **LB + DB (Coverage period)**
  - Hook/curl + flat coordination.
  - Seam/vertical carry rules.
  - Pattern-matching vs 2x2 or 3x1 sets.
  - “Pass off” drills for crossing routes.

- **DL + LB + DB (Blitz communication)**
  - Blitz path recognition (LB timing behind DL slant).
  - Coverage rotation behind pressure.
  - Contain integrity on edge blitzes.

#### Defensive Indi Period Structure

##### Overview
The Defensive Individual (Indi) period builds the foundation of your defense. Each position group focuses on the fundamentals that directly tie into that day’s defensive install — whether it’s a front, coverage, or pressure package.  
The goal: every player understands their assignment, alignment, and technique so when team periods start, they can play fast and confident.

---

#### Heavy Tackle Session

Before breaking into defensive individual periods, this is a great time to run a **Heavy Tackle Session**.  
This session focuses on tackling fundamentals in a controlled, high-rep environment. It allows players to build confidence and technique before applying it in live or team settings.

##### Goals
- Reinforce **safe and consistent tackling technique**
- Improve **tracking angles** and **contact confidence**
- Teach players to **trust their leverage and pursuit**
- Build team-wide **physicality and effort**

##### Structure
Each station should emphasize **form, control, and repetition**. Rotate through stations every 4–6 minutes depending on team size.

###### 1. **Tracking & Leverage**
- Focus: Approach under control, maintain leverage (inside shoulder free).
- Drills:
  - **Angle Tackle Tracking Drill** – Defender closes space with proper leverage.
  - **Mirror & Close Drill** – Match runner’s hips and break down under control.

###### 2. **Hawk Roll / Form Tackle**
- Focus: Wrap, roll, and finish safely.
- Drills:
  - **Seahawks Tackling Progression** – Focus on “eyes through the thighs,” wrap, and roll.
  - **Close-Quarter Fit Drill** – Partner up; fit up under control on command.

###### 3. **Open-Field Tackling**
- Focus: Balance, patience, and finishing in space.
- Drills:
  - **Open-Field Box Drill** – Runner must stay within the box, defender squares up and tracks hips.
  - **Angle Tackle on Cone** – Emphasize closing space and attacking at proper depth.

###### 4. **Contact + Finish**
- Focus: Hit on the rise, drive feet, and finish through.
- Drills:
  - **Shoulder Pop Drill (Pads Only)** – Contact with shoulder and wrap.
  - **Tackle and Squeeze Drill** – Finish with drive steps and body control.

> **Coaching Tips**
> - Always start slow and controlled — form first, then add speed.
> - Emphasize eyes up, shoulder leverage, and finish.
> - Reinforce communication: “Ball! Ball! Ball!” on contact.
> - Reward good form and effort over big hits.

Once tackling fundamentals have been reinforced through this session, transition directly into defensive individual periods.

#### Team Defense Period

This period brings all 11 together to execute the full defensive install with game-like tempo and structure.

**Goals**
- Master alignment, assignment, and pursuit.
- Communicate pre-snap adjustments.
- Build confidence through repetition and clear expectations.
- Execute as one — “11 hats to the football.”

**Suggested Structure**
1. **Alignment Walk-Through (10 min)**  
   - Line up vs common formations.  
   - Call adjustments out loud (motion, shift, trips check).  
   - Coach corrects eyes, stance, and leverage.

2. **Fit Tempo Period (10–12 min)**  
   - Half-speed run fits and coverage assignments.  
   - Emphasis: clean transitions, gap integrity, pursuit lanes.

3. **Full-Speed Team (15–20 min)**  
   - Execute live calls vs scout offense.  
   - Coach effort, communication, and pursuit more than perfection.  
   - Track tackling angles and run fits.

4. **Situational Defense (5–10 min)**  
   - 3rd down, red zone, goal line, and 2-minute situations.  
   - Emphasize awareness and discipline under pressure.

---

### Coaching Notes / Tips
- Defensive Indi is about repetition and reaction — build muscle memory.  
- Keep players moving; avoid long lines.  
- Always coach “eyes first” — where a defender looks determines how they move.  
- Reinforce team pursuit: every player must finish near the ball.  
- Build daily themes: one takeaway, one pursuit focus, one tackling emphasis.  
- Encourage defensive communication — loud, confident, and constant.  
- Use film or live demos to connect drills to game situations.

> "Defense isn't about guessing right — it's about reacting faster, hitting harder, and trusting your teammates."  
> — Coach Kyle

## Offensive Practice Notes

- Keep Indi periods focused and short — high quality reps matter more than long reps.
- Use simple language and landmarks (e.g., "playside armpit," "post-snap anchor") so players can internalize quickly.
- When installing new plays, increase repetitions for core techniques (pulling, mesh timing, MOR).
- Rotate coaches to different groups so instruction is consistent and varied.
- Track reps for key assignments (pullers, mesh exchanges, QB drop counts) to ensure players get the necessary work.
