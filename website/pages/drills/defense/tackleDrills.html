Tackle Drills

Author: Coach Kyle | Created: June 03, 2025

Tackling must be taught with an emphasis on safety, technique, and control. These drills focus on proper form—keeping the head up, wrapping up, and driving through the ball carrier—while building confidence and reinforcing good habits.

The Proper Tackling Stance

4 Key points: Feet shoulder width, squeeze shoulder blades, sink (bend knees/waist), and hands ready for uppercuts.

Near Foot Drill

Explode into the near foot stance on command. Leading with the correct foot is essential for leverage.

Swoop (Breakdown Position)

Approach the ball-carrier with the appropriate lead foot based on leverage. Freeze in place at the end of the bag.

Shoot Drill

Sink at the waist and explode forward while throwing uppercuts. Keep head up and cleats out of the grass.

Upper Cuts

Drive for 5 yards using uppercuts. Can be done with a large exercise ball to vary angles. Start with near hip near shoulder, then drive through. Athlete should be making contact with the near shoulder, and driving with the same leg through the target.

Near Foot Near Shoulder

Two players stand 5 yards apart. Tackler leads with near foot and touches with near shoulder-side hand.

Near Hip Tracking

Tackler tracks the runner through cones, watching the runner's hip and maintaining correct foot lead.

Run & Gather

Mirror the ball carrier and gather into a swoop when they sink their hips to stay balanced.

Pop-Up Tackle

Player starts on the ground and explodes up into proper tackle position on command. Builds reactive quickness and reinforces low pad level at the moment of contact.

Form Tackle Progression

Complete sequence: Run, Swoop, Upper cuts, Drive for five, Head up for vision.

All Blacks Rugby - Tackle Form

Excellent cross-sport example of safe and effective tackling mechanics.

Low Impact Tackling

Training safe contact without high-velocity collisions.

Angle & Sprint Tackles

Form tackling with resistance. Bag carrier walks or sprints at various angles away from the tackler.

Tight Angle Tackle

Defender closes at a sharp angle to cut off the ball carrier's path. Emphasizes leverage and near-foot alignment at the point of contact.

Kneeling Drive Drills

Start in a kneeling position and explode into proper upper-cut drive contact. Progressions move from a basic drive to a roll entry, then add pads. Isolates the hip explosion and wrap mechanics before introducing footwork.

Kneeling Drive vs. Player

Advances the kneeling drive into live contact with a partner. Teaches safe contact entry from the ground up before introducing a full-speed running approach.

Standing Drive Progression

From a standing start, player explodes into tackle position and drives through the bag. Progress from a bare drive to the full progression, then add pads. Bridges the gap between kneeling and two-step work.

Standing Roll Progression

Player rolls into a standing drive position, then executes a tackle on the bag. Develops balance and reaction in the approach. Progress from no pads to pads, then to live partner contact.

Two-Step Drive Progression

Two-step approach before exploding into tackle contact. Adds controlled footwork to the drive sequence. Includes a roll-entry variation with pads for advanced reps.

Running Drive Progression

Full running approach into tackle contact. Players must show proper form at speed before adding pads. The final stage in the drive progression sequence—kneeling, standing, two-step, then running.

Two-Man Tackling Drills

Partner-based drills focusing on timing, pursuit angles, and coordinated contact. Includes standard reps and the rabbit variation where the defender must chase and track before making contact.

Tackle Tube: Leverage Step and Drive

Take 2–3 steps before landing on tube. Emphasizes near foot/shoulder lead.

Tackle Tube Tuesday: Cornerback/Safety

Half-field tackling drill for secondary players using the tackle tube.

Linebacker Fill Drill

Simulate OL gaps. LB must scan gap before attacking the rolled tube. Shoulder lead depends on runner direction.
